The lead: switch expiry style by recent results

On each expiry day, trade the champion's breakout if its last 6 expiry days added up to at least what the three fades made over the same days; otherwise trade the fades. 83 expiry days with real option bars, causal prices. Cumulative sum of daily results in %.

  • Lookbacks of 4 and 8 expiries give +1,178 and +1,153, so the result does not hang on the choice of 6.
  • Its gain over the champion has t 4.23 on the daily difference. It beats 99.9 % of random switches with the same share of fade days, and all 2,000 shuffles of the day order. The edge is persistence: an expiry style that has been working keeps working for weeks.
  • Why it is not live: I found it after the agents finished, on all the data, so it was not pre-registered. It still loses on the judged half. It replaces rather than adds on expiry days, and you chose to keep the champion trading them.
  • Next step: a shadow account that runs a three-fade expiry book next to the champion and applies the switch, so it builds a live record before you decide.

What the night established

  • Price-only entries (fades, breakouts, opening ranges, regimes, models) showed no edge that held from one year to the next. Several looked strong on one or two splits; none held on all four.
  • The account is decided by expiry days, and the right expiry style flips between breakout and fade as the market changes regime.
  • Time-of-day structure is real. 09:15 to 09:45 loses for every entry. On normal days ATM IV falls 5 to 10 % through the session; on expiry afternoons it rises 12 to 27 %. No rule built on this made money out of sample.
  • Fade trades want different exits: a 25 % stop, no forward trail and a 45-minute clock. On random entries the champion's exit ladder alone costs about 1 % a trade.
